🔒 Everything runs in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere. All signals are read by JavaScript from standard browser APIs on this device — no network requests are made. This demo shows exactly what a third-party script on any web page can learn about you.

What "bits of entropy" means here: Each signal contributes an estimate of how many bits of information it carries about a particular browser. 33 bits ≈ 1 in 8 billion; 20 bits ≈ 1 in 1 million. These estimates are rough and illustrative — real uniqueness depends on the population of browsers you compare against (e.g., EFF Panopticlick data).

The total is a rough lower bound — many signals are correlated, so the effective entropy is lower. This is a teaching illustration, not a surveillance tool.

Real uniqueness benchmark: 33 bits ≈ 1 in 8 billion browsers; 20 bits ≈ 1 in 1 million. The EFF Panopticlick study (2010) found the median browser carried ~18.9 bits. Modern browsers with privacy protections (Firefox, Brave, Safari ITP) reduce this significantly.
