What you are actually doing when you clean
  • Keep the raw file. Every fix here is reversible only if you do.
  • Record the reason for each change. Six months later, "it looked wrong" tells you nothing.
  • The same blank can come from a broken sensor or from someone refusing to answer. You cannot tell which from the file.
That is why the rest of this module works backwards from causes instead of forwards from tools.

The file · 4 of 4

Four years are absent, and no column shows it Every check so far asked: given a row, is this cell blank? That question cannot reach a row that was never written.

Years requested vs years returned
The scraper asked for 2000–2025. Four years returned an empty payload and it wrote nothing rather than raising. The file is short, and being short leaves no trace.

Two checks that catch this

  1. Ask which years came back. Subtract what you got from what you requested. 
    set(range(2000, 2026)) - set(df.year)
    {2000, 2001, 2002, 2003}
  2. Count the rows in each year. A trading year holds about 250 days, so a short year or a zero shows up at once. 
    df.groupby("year").size()
    2004 254 22 years 2005 251 min 243, max 254 ... none short, none zero 2025 249 the four gaps are absent, not short

And a column that is not data

RequestNumber is the server's pagination handle. It describes your scrape, and it leaks that the file came from 22 separate requests.

Mechanisms · 1 of 5

The cause decides the fix A column is 35% empty. What do you do? There is no answer until you say why.

The observation

A numeric column. 5,463 rows, 1,932 blank. Nothing else known.

No method is correct here. Whether a method works depends on what caused the gap, and you have not been told.
Rubin's 1976 framework names the three causes.
If the cause wasThe honest response
The logger dropped packets at random Drop the rows or fill from the column. Estimates stay unbiased. You lose precision, nothing else.
The feed only carried this field after a certain date Fill nothing. Restrict the analysis, or use a column that spans the history. Filling here invents eight years of prices.
Values were suppressed when they were extreme Nothing from the observed rows is safe, the mean included. You need an assumption from outside the data, and must report how the answer moves with it.
Same cells, same count, three obligations. Fill all three with the column mean and you get one clean-looking file and two wrong answers.

Exit polls · the same three mechanisms, in words

MCAR, MAR, MNAR in an exit poll Outside a polling booth on counting day. You ask who they voted for, plus age, gender, income. Some refuse.

MechanismWhy they did not answer What it does to your number
MCAR Late for work, bus arriving. 5% of forms blow away. Costs sample size only. The wind does not know how they voted, so your vote share stays centred on the truth.
MAR 30% of under-25s skip the vote question, 5% of everyone else. Within one age group, refusing has nothing to do with the vote. Biased, and fixable. You know everyone's age, refusers included, so you know how much each group is under-counted. Reweight, or model vote on age.
MNAR Party X voters are embarrassed and refuse at ten times the rate. Demographically they are ordinary. Biased, not fixable. Reweighting does nothing: the distributions already match. Imputing is worse, since the model learns the pattern the refusers do not follow.
For MNAR you bound it, you do not solve it. Assume the refusers voted X with probability p, redo the analysis across a plausible range, report the range. For MNAR that range is the answer.
Why exit polls. The truth arrives hours later, when the votes are counted. Almost no survey lets you check your bias against reality.
Exit polls · what the wrong assumption cost

2016: a MAR correction applied to MNAR missingness Standard methods, competent weighting, wrong in a direction the mechanism predicts.

  1. Response rates were lower among rural, non-college-educated white voters, who broke heavily for Trump.
  2. Pollsters reweighted by age, gender, race, region. Correct procedure, if the missingness is MAR given those variables.
  3. Education was under-weighted or omitted in many state polls, and it was the variable most tied to both response and vote.
  4. The residual missingness was MNAR. Willingness to answer tracked the vote itself.
  5. Reweighting redistributes people you have. It cannot conjure people whose absence is caused by the answer they would have given.
Every published margin tilted the same way. Not by accident. One shared assumption was wrong in one shared direction.

The part that generalises

A missing-data assumption is a claim about the world. When it is wrong, every downstream number moves together. Correlated error does not average out across polls, models or teams, which is why the industry missed it together.

What changed after. Education entered the weighting scheme as standard, and sensitivity analysis over non-response became normal reporting. Neither fixes the problem. Both show how far the answer could move.

Mechanisms · 4 of 5 · the central difficulty

MAR and MNAR look the same until you condition Both slope against age, and both slope against their own hidden values. What separates them is whether the relationship survives conditioning on the observed columns.

% missing within each age band, split by the row's true value
Income is MAR. Inside an age band, earning above or below the median makes no difference to answering. Health is MNAR. The low scorers are withheld five to ten times as often, in every band.
This chart cannot be drawn from real data. It splits rows by a value that is missing for exactly the rows being counted. It exists here only because the data was simulated.

Two patterns, not eight. The three price columns never go missing separately. That is a format change, not dropped readings.

The verdict

Missingness is a deterministic function of the date. A logistic regression of OPEN.isna() on date separates the classes perfectly, training accuracy 1.0. Depends on observed data only, so this is MAR.

The cause is knowable, which beats a classification. The exchange only began publishing open, high and low in October 2011. Nothing went missing. Look the cause up first: it is faster than a test and answers a better question.
Mechanisms · the sting

MAR does not mean fillable The file is MAR. Every textbook says MAR is where imputation works. Here it does not work at all.

There is no overlap. To learn a rule for OPEN from date you need dates where OPEN is sometimes present and sometimes missing. Every date is one or the other. The predictor that explains the missingness perfectly leaves nothing to learn from.
If you did it anywayWhat you get
df.OPEN.fillna(df.OPEN.mean()) Eight years of opens set to a later average, on an index that went from a base of 1,000 to 11,168. Every one wrong by a factor.
Regress OPEN on CLOSE, fit on 2011+ An R² of 0.999 and a comfortable feeling. You extrapolated a modern intraday relationship into a period with different circuit rules and constituents. The fit statistic sees none of that.
Forward-fill from the previous row There is no previous row. The gap runs to the start of the file.

Do this instead

Restrict the window. If the analysis needs OHLC it starts 2011-10-03, and you say so. Or change the measure. CLOSE is complete across all 5,463 rows, so daily returns keep the full history and impute nothing.

The rule. The mechanism says whether filling is defensible. Overlap says whether it is possible. Structural gaps are MAR and un-imputable at once, and they are everywhere in scraped data.
Carry this into Lecture 3. Every method there returns numbers for this column. None of them warns you. The check has to be yours.

What you may do before Lecture 3

Deletion and its assumptions It is the default in most libraries, and most people never notice they chose it.

ApproachWhat it doesValid when
Listwise
df.dropna()		 Drop any row with a gap anywhere. Every analysis runs on the same rows. On the survey, 400 becomes 200.MCAR
Pairwise
df.corr()		 Use every row that has the columns each statistic needs. Keeps more data, but each number rests on a different subsample.MCAR
Column deletion Drop the variable. Honest for Index Name at 100% missing and INDEX_NAME at one distinct value.Any
Restrict the window Drop a whole defined region, with a stated reason. The answer for the pre-2011 block.Structural
Delete nothing
df.OPEN.isna()		 Keep every row, add the missingness indicator as a column. Under MNAR the absence itself carries information.Any
The default you did not choose. scikit-learn raises on NaN. A groupby or mean() drops rows quietly. Pairwise deletion is pandas' default, carrying an MCAR assumption.
df = pd.read_csv("BLUE METAL.csv", na_values=["-"])
len(df), len(df.dropna()), len(df.dropna(subset=["OPEN"]))
(5463, 0, 3531)
An empty DataFrame, no error. One column is 100% null, so every row goes. Drop it first and the same call keeps 3,531. Listwise deletion is only as good as your column list.
Report two numbers when you delete: how many rows went, and whether the survivors resemble the departed. On the survey, mean age moves 40.9 to 40.0.
Two per cent missing, no structure, no difference between kept and dropped? Delete, note it, move on.

Recap

What to take from this lecture Six things, in the order they will save you time.

01The reported missing count can be wrong

On this file it was off by 2.1×. Check dtypes, hunt sentinels, and re-read with na_values before reporting a number.

02Look for rows that were never written

Four missing years left no trace in any column. Compare what came back against what you asked for.

03The mechanism decides the method

MCAR depends on nothing, MAR on what you observed, MNAR on what you did not. Choose wrongly and every downstream number moves the same way at once.

04MAR and MNAR look alike in your data

Both slope against observed variables. The difference shows only against the hidden values, the one comparison you can never run.

05MAR does not mean fillable

A predictor that explains the gaps perfectly can leave the model with no rows to learn from. Ask what it has to fit before reaching for a method that answers regardless.

06Documentation beats inference

A logistic regression classified this file as MAR. One line in the exchange's release notes explained it.

Practice · and what comes next

Before Lecture 2 Four exercises on today's two files.

  1. Profile a file you scraped yourself. Report every column's dtype, the non-numeric values in each numeric column, the number of distinct missingness patterns, and rows expected against rows received.
  2. Find the sentinel. Write a function returning, per object column, the values that will not parse as numbers, by frequency. Sentinels are the few very frequent ones.
  3. Break the diagnosis. Shuffle the rows of survey.csv and redraw the matrix plot. Explain what was lost.
  4. Argue a mechanism. Pick one column with gaps in your own data. Four sentences: what caused them, which mechanism that implies, what evidence supports it, and what evidence would change your mind. The fourth is the one that matters.
