Edit distance · the word

Concept The Levenshtein distance between two strings is the fewest single-letter edits that turn one into the other.

editexamplecost
insert a letterkavita → kavitha1
delete a letterpriyaa → priya1
substitute a lettervijai → vijay1
similarity = 1 − distance / length of the longer string kavita, kavitha: 1 − 1/7 = 0.857

Damerau

Adds a fourth edit: swap two neighbouring letters. "fahtima" is one swap from "fathima".

Six slides build the table that computes it.
Edit distance · step 1 of 6

Intuition kitten becomes sitting in three edits: two substitutions and one insertion. The distance is the smallest such count.

kitten over sitting, letter by letter
k → s: one substitution.
e → i: a second substitution.
Insert g at the end. Three edits, and no route uses fewer.
Finding the fewest by trying every route is hopeless for long strings. A table does it.
Edit distance · step 2 of 6

Intuition continued 1 Lay kitten down the side and sitting across the top. Cell (i, j) holds the fewest edits that turn the first i letters of kitten into the first j letters of sitting.

one row per prefix of kitten, one column per prefix of sitting
∅ is the empty string: no letters yet.
The cell at row "t", column "t" asks: how many edits turn "kit" into "sit"?
Prefixes "kit" and "sit", boxed. Every cell is a smaller version of the whole question.
The bottom-right cell asks the whole question: kitten against sitting.
Edit distance · step 3 of 6

Intuition continued 2 Each step along row ∅ inserts one letter of sitting. Each step down column ∅ deletes one letter of kitten. Both cost one edit.

the border: distance from the empty string
Row ∅: nothing into s, si, sit, …. Each cell is the cell to its left + 1: insert the next letter of sitting.
Column ∅: k, ki, kit, … into nothing. Each cell is the cell above + 1: delete the next letter of kitten.
The same two moves work inside the table. "ki" into "s" from above: "k" into "s" costs 1, then delete i. Total 2.
The third move is the diagonal: line up the last two letters. Free if they match, 1 edit if not.
Edit distance · step 4 of 6

Intuition continued 3 One cell is the smallest of three moves: from above plus 1, from the left plus 1, or the diagonal plus 0 if the letters match and 1 if not.

cell (k, s): one letter of each
cell(i, j) = min( above + 1, left + 1, diag + (0 if same letter else 1) )
From above: 1 + 1 = 2.
From the left: 1 + 1 = 2.
Diagonal: 0 + 1 = 1, since k and s differ.
The smallest is 1: "k" to "s" is one substitution.
Edit distance · step 5 of 6

Intuition continued 4 Apply the rule left to right, row by row. Each row needs only the row above it.

the table filled one row of kitten at a time
Row k: every entry is at least 1, since s ≠ k.
Row i: the diagonal i = i costs nothing, giving 1.
Rows t, t: the diagonal of matches stays at 1.
Row e: e meets i, a second substitution, giving 2.
Row n: n = n, and the last column adds the inserted g: 3.
Edit distance · step 6 of 6

Intuition continued 5 The bottom-right cell is the distance: 3. Tracing back the cells that gave each minimum recovers the three edits.

the finished table, with the path of edits
d(kitten, sitting) = 3 similarity = 1 − 3/7 = 0.571
Red cells: the route k→s, keep i t t, e→i, keep n, insert g.
The table has 7 × 8 cells. Two names of length m and n cost m × n steps.
Damerau-Levenshtein adds one move, the swap of two neighbours, so "fahtima" to "fathima" costs 1.

Jaro-Winkler · the word

Concept Jaro-Winkler counts letters the two strings share near the same position, penalises those out of order, and adds a bonus for a common start.

jaro = ( m/|s1| + m/|s2| + (m − t)/m ) / 3 jw = jaro + ℓ × p × (1 − jaro) m matching letters t half the matched letters out of order ℓ common prefix, at most 4 p 0.1

Range

0 for nothing shared, 1 for identical.

Built for names

Designed at the US Census Bureau for matching person records.

Six slides compute it for MARTHA and MARHTA.
Jaro-Winkler · step 1 of 6

Intuition Two letters match only if they are equal and close: at most ⌊max(6, 6) / 2⌋ − 1 = 2 positions apart.

the match window for one letter
window = ⌊max(|s1|, |s2|) / 2⌋ − 1 = ⌊6 / 2⌋ − 1 = 2
The T at position 3 of MARTHA may match any T at positions 1 to 5 of MARHTA.
A letter far away is chance agreement, so it does not count.
Jaro-Winkler · step 2 of 6

Intuition continued 1 Walk along s1 and pair each letter with the first unused equal letter of s2 inside the window. All 6 letters find a partner.

each letter of MARTHA joined to its match in MARHTA
M, A, R match in place.
T matches the T one place right; H matches the H one place left.
A matches in place. m = 6.
Jaro-Winkler · step 3 of 6

Intuition continued 2 Read the matched letters of each string in order: MARTHA and MARHTA. They disagree at 2 positions, so t = 1.

the crossing pair is a transposition
s1 matched, in order: M A R T H A s2 matched, in order: M A R H T A out of order: 2 → t = 1
Crossing lines are the transposition. Each swap is counted once, hence the half.
Jaro-Winkler · step 4 of 6

Intuition continued 3 Average three shares: matched letters of s1, matched letters of s2, and matches that are in order.

all six matched
jaro = ( m/|s1| + m/|s2| + (m − t)/m ) / 3
symbolmeaninghere
|s1|, |s2|length of each string6, 6
mletters matched inside the window, step 26
thalf the matched letters out of order, step 31
termmeasureshere
m/|s1|share of s1 that found a match6/6 = 1.000
m/|s2|share of s2 that found a match6/6 = 1.000
(m − t)/mshare of matches in the same order5/6 = 0.833
jaro = ( 6/6 + 6/6 + (6 − 1)/6 ) / 3 = ( 1 + 1 + 0.833 ) / 3 = 0.944
The swap costs 0.056. Levenshtein charges the same swap 2 edits out of 6: similarity 0.667.
Jaro-Winkler · step 5 of 6

Intuition continued 4 Winkler adds a bonus for a common start of ℓ letters, up to 4, scaled by p = 0.1. MAR gives ℓ = 3.

the common prefix
jw = jaro + ℓ × p × (1 − jaro) = 0.944 + 3 × 0.1 × 0.056 = 0.961
The bonus closes 30% of the remaining distance to 1.
rapidfuzz adds the bonus only when jaro is above 0.7.

Tokens and sound · the word

Concept Jaccard compares the sets of words or letter pairs two strings contain. Soundex compares a code for how a name sounds.

Jaccard: shared items over all items, J = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|
A set has no order, so "kumar ram" and "ram kumar" score 1.
Soundex: a first letter and 3 digits
Metaphone: the consonant sounds
Lakshmi and Laxmi share 2 of 8 letter pairs, and both code to L250.
Caroline and Karolynn sound the same and share 3 of 11 letter pairs. Soundex keeps the first letter, so they code to C645 and K645.
Metaphone writes the sound, so C before a becomes K. Both names code to KRLN.

Fellegi-Sunter · the word

Concept Compare a pair on every column. Each comparison gets a weight from how often it happens for the same patient and for different patients. Add the weights.

m = P(column agrees | same patient) u = P(column agrees | different patients) agree: weight = log₂( m / u ) disagree: weight = log₂( (1 − m) / (1 − u) ) total = sum over columns

Fellegi and Sunter, 1969

A theory of record linkage written for census and health records.

Bits of evidence

A weight of +1 doubles the odds that the pair is one patient; −1 halves them.

Six slides build it on one column, then six.
Fellegi-Sunter · step 1 of 6

Intuition Among pairs that are one patient, phones agree 95.6% of the time. Among different patients, 4 in a million. The ratio is the evidence.

phone, agreement rate
m = 0.9564 u = 0.000004 m / u = 239,100 weight = log₂(m / u) = +17.79
A phone match is 239,100 times likelier for the same patient. That is +17.79 bits.
Where m and u come from is the Mechanism slide. Take them as given for now.
Fellegi-Sunter · step 2 of 6

Intuition continued 1 Disagreement is evidence too. Same-patient phones differ 4.4% of the time, so a different phone costs -4.52 bits, a small penalty.

phone, the two outcomes
People change numbers, so disagreement is weak evidence against.
Agreement on a rare value is strong evidence for. The weights are lopsided.
Fellegi-Sunter · step 3 of 6

Intuition continued 2 Add the six columns. If the columns agree independently within each group, adding log ratios multiplies the evidence. J. Rajesh and Rajesh total +54.90.

rec 1054 "J. Rajesh" against rec 1247 "Rajesh"
Name: "j rajesh" against "rajesh", Jaro-Winkler 0.86, the middle level.
Dob and phone agree: +14.01 and +17.79.
Address and pincode agree; "Rd" became "road".
The total is a log likelihood ratio, the same additive form as the independence step of Little's test in lecture 2.

Fellegi-Sunter · where m and u come from

Mechanism EM estimates m and the share of matches from the candidate pairs alone. It settles at λ = 0.0160: 200 expected matches against 187 true.

1. guess m (agree 90% for a match), λ = 0.05 2. E-step: for each pair, P(match | its pattern) 3. Blocking · the word

Concept Blocking compares only pairs that share a key. Pairs in different blocks are never scored.

records n = 508 pairs = n(n − 1) / 2 = 128,778 1 lakh records: pairs = 4,999,950,000

Key

A cheap value that duplicates share: pincode, a sound code of the name, the last digits of the phone.

Union of keys

Run several keys and score a pair if any key puts it together.

Six slides measure what each key keeps and what it loses.
