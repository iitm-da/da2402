Keys · Concept

Concept A primary key names one row of a table. A foreign key in another table stores that name, which links the two rows.

ecom.db, three tables and two links

Primary key

customer_id is unique and never empty. Every other table refers to a customer by it.

Foreign key

orders.customer_id must hold a customer_id that exists in customers.

UNIQUE

Two customers cannot share an email, even though email is not the key.

Keys · Mechanism: RESTRICT and CASCADE

Mechanism continued 1 With foreign keys on, ON DELETE RESTRICT refuses to delete a product that has orders. ON UPDATE CASCADE rewrites the orders when a product id changes.

Keys · Drawbacks: types are suggestions

Drawbacks A column declared REAL accepted the text cheap. SQLite converts a value to the column type when it can and stores it as it is when it cannot.

INSERT INTO products VALUES (60, 'Test',  'cheap', 'Snacks');
INSERT INTO products VALUES (61, 'Test2', '45',    'Snacks');
SELECT product_id, price, typeof(price) FROM products
WHERE product_id IN (1, 60, 61);
output
(1, 90.0, 'real') (60, 'cheap', 'text') (61, 45.0, 'real')

Type affinity

'45' looks like a number and is stored as 45.0. 'cheap' does not, and is stored as text in a REAL column.

After the read

pd.read_sql on this column returns object, the same symptom as a CSV in lecture 1.

STRICT tables

SQLite 3.37 added tables that refuse wrong types. The sqlite3 on the lecture laptop is 3.31.1.

Queries · Concept

Concept A query is written starting with SELECT and run starting with FROM. The run order explains which names each clause can use.

the clauses of one SELECT

WHERE runs before GROUP BY

So WHERE filters rows and cannot test COUNT(*). HAVING runs after grouping and can.

SELECT runs late

An alias made in SELECT, such as AS customers, is usable in ORDER BY and not in WHERE.

Joins · Drawbacks

  
Drawbacks
    A join multiplies rows: 100 customers joined to their orders become 4,000 rows. A count or sum taken after the join counts the joined rows.

  


SELECT COUNT(*) FROM customers c
JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;
output
4000
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) FROM customers c
JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;
output
94
Count what you mean
Customers who ordered: 94, with DISTINCT. Rows after the join: 4,000.
No RIGHT or FULL JOIN here
SQLite added them in 3.39. On 3.31, swap the tables and use LEFT JOIN.

  






  
Window functions · Concept

  
Concept
    A window function computes over many rows without collapsing them. SUM(...) OVER () gives every product row the grand total beside its own.

  


SELECT p.product_id, p.product_name,
  SUM(p.price) AS revenue,
  ROUND(100 * SUM(p.price)
        / SUM(SUM(p.price)) OVER (), 2)
    AS pct_of_total
FROM orders o
JOIN products p ON p.product_id = o.product_id
GROUP BY p.product_id, p.product_name
ORDER BY revenue DESC, p.product_id
LIMIT 5;
share of all revenue, ₹550,240 over 4,000 orders
output

Window functions · Mechanism

  
Mechanism
    The inner SUM runs per product, inside GROUP BY. The outer SUM(...) OVER () then adds those 47 sums across the whole result.

  


stepwhat it computesGulab Jamun
1  GROUP BY productone row per product sold: 47 rows284 orders at ₹555
2  SUM(p.price)revenue of that product₹157,620
3  SUM(SUM(p.price)) OVER ()the sum of step 2 over all 47 rows, written on every row₹550,240
4  100 × step 2 / step 3share of total28.65%
OVER ()
Empty brackets mean one window: the whole result. OVER (PARTITION BY description) would give the total per product type instead.
NULLIF in 2025
The MySQL query divided by NULLIF(total, 0) to avoid dividing by zero on an empty table. With 4,000 orders the guard never fires, and the slide drops it.

Governance · Example 2: erasing a customer

  
Example continued 1
    Customer 1 asks to be erased. RESTRICT refuses the delete because of 73 orders, and the shop has to choose what happens to them.

  


DELETE FROM customers WHERE customer_id = 1;
output
sqlite3.IntegrityError: FOREIGN KEY constraint failed
Option 1: delete the orders too
DELETE FROM orders    WHERE customer_id = 1;   -- 73 rows
DELETE FROM customers WHERE customer_id = 1;   -- 1 row
Option 2: keep the orders, remove the person
UPDATE customers
SET name = 'erased', email = 'erased-1@invalid', place = NULL
WHERE customer_id = 1;
Option 1 rewrites history
Total revenue falls from ₹550,240 to ₹541,660. Every past report on 2024 and 2025 changes.
Option 2 keeps the sales
The 73 orders stay, pointing at a row with the name, email and place removed.
RESTRICT turned a silent deletion into a decision someone had to make and write down.
