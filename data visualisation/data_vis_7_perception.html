Preattentive attributes · Mechanism: feature integration

Mechanism Treisman and Gelade (1980): single features are registered across the whole field at once; a conjunction needs attention to move from item to item.

feature integration theory

One feature

A red item among blue ones is found in parallel. Adding more blue items costs little time.

Conjunction

A red square among red circles and blue squares shares each feature with some distractors. The eye checks items one at a time, so time grows with the number of items.

Preattentive attributes · Drawbacks

Drawbacks A preattentive feature marks one thing. Using two of them for two variables puts the reader back into a conjunction search.

One highlight per chart

Colour India, and India pops out. Colour India and Brazil and China in three hues, and each needs a legend lookup.

Features compete

Colour by continent and shape by income group on one scatter: finding "rich Asian countries" is a colour + shape conjunction.

Hues run out

Each extra category needs a hue the reader can tell apart. Palettes and colour blindness are lecture 10.

Lecture 8 uses the same features for grouping: similarity of colour and shape makes items read as one group.
Encoding accuracy · Concept: the Cleveland-McGill ranking

Concept Cleveland and McGill (1984) ranked the elementary tasks a reader performs to decode a number from a chart, from most to least accurate.

elementary perceptual tasks, ranked
Source: W. S. Cleveland and R. McGill, Graphical perception, Journal of the American Statistical Association 79 (1984). Their experiments tested a subset of these tasks; the rest of the order is their argument from other findings.

Encoding accuracy · Mechanism: scoring a judgement

Mechanism Each answer is scored by the log of its absolute error in percentage points, with 1/8 added so that an exact answer does not give log of zero.

error = log2( |judged % − true %| + 1/8 )
answerabsolute errorscore
judged 60, true 50|60 − 50| = 10log₂(10.125) = 3.34
judged 52, true 50|52 − 50| = 2log₂(2.125) = 1.09
judged 50, true 500log₂(0.125) = -3.00
judged 90, true 5040log₂(40.125) = 5.33

Why a log

An error of 40 points scores 5.33, not four times the 10-point score of 3.34. One wild guess does not swamp an encoding's average.

Why 1/8

It sets the floor at −3 for an exact answer and keeps every score finite.

Same measure in the replication

Heer and Bostock (2010) scored their crowdsourced judgements with this formula, so the two studies compare directly.

Encoding accuracy · Example: the replication

Example Heer and Bostock (2010) reran the position, length and angle judgements with Amazon Mechanical Turk workers and recovered the same order.

The 1984 study

Subjects judged percentages on printed charts in two experiments: position against angle (bars and pies) and position against length.

The 2010 replication

Online workers judged the same kinds of charts in a browser. Position on a common scale again came first and angle below length.

Added in 2010

Area judgements on circles and rectangles ranked below position and length, as the 1984 ordering placed them.

Encoding accuracy · Mechanism: Weber's law

Mechanism continued 1 Weber's law: the smallest difference a person can detect is a fixed fraction of the magnitude. A common baseline turns a long-length comparison into a short one.

bar A = 100, bar B = 100 + d browser JS
Set d to 2 or 3 and compare the two layouts. Non-aligned, the eye compares two whole lengths. Aligned, it reads a short overhang past the gridline.
Cleveland and McGill used this argument for why position on a common scale beats length; it is also why the middle segments of a stacked bar are hard.
Encoding accuracy · Mechanism: Stevens' power law

Mechanism continued 2 Perceived size grows as actual size raised to a power. For length the power is close to 1; for area studies report about 0.7, so a bubble 4.4 times larger reads as about 2.8 times.

perceived = actual ^ exponent

Length

Twice as long looks twice as long. Bars and dots inherit this.

Area

China's population is 4.38 times that of the US. An area-scaled bubble shows 4.38×, and with an exponent of 0.7 it reads as 2.81×.

The area exponent varies by study and by shape; values from about 0.6 to 0.9 are reported. 0.7 is the value attributed to S. S. Stevens.
Encoding accuracy · Assumptions

Assumptions The ranking measures one task: reading the ratio of two values. It holds for that task and for readers like the subjects tested.

the ranking assumeswhich holds whenand fails when
the reader compares two valuesAfrica against the Americasthe reader wants the overall shape, such as a trend over 12 years
accuracy is the goala report where numbers drive a decisionthe chart only has to show that Asia is the largest
one encoding per valuea bar charta bubble chart uses position, area and colour at once
readers with typical vision and time to looka lecture or a reporta dashboard glanced at for a second, or a colour-blind reader
Encoding accuracy · Drawbacks

Drawbacks Position is the best channel and there are two of them, x and y. Every further variable goes to a channel lower down the ranking.

The bubble chart of lecture 7

x = GDP per capita (position), y = life expectancy (position), size = population (area, rank 4), colour = continent (hue). Two variables get accurate channels; two do not.

Small multiples reuse position

Facets by continent give each panel its own x and y. Comparing across panels drops to rank 2, non-aligned scales.

Silent on grouping

The ranking does not say which items read as one group. That is gestalt, lecture 8.

Silent on colour choice

Saturation ranks last for reading values; which palette to use is lecture 10.

Pattern perception · Concept: detection, assembly, estimation

Concept Cleveland (1985) splits reading a graph into three steps: detect the data, assemble it into patterns, estimate values. The ranking so far covered estimation.

gapminder 2007, 142 countries, the same scatter three times
Detection fails when symbols vanish into grids or into each other. Estimation also covers slopes, gaps between curves and the strength of a cloud, each taken up next.
W. S. Cleveland, The Elements of Graphing Data (1985; revised 1994). Assembly is the grouping of lecture 8.

Detection · Mechanism: what overlap leaves visible

Mechanism Two symbols 0 to 6 px apart, drawn six times larger. A filled circle covers the whole disc under it; a ring or a plus covers only its strokes.

symbol radius 4.5 px, enlarged 6 times; the later symbol drawn dark
At 2 px apart the earlier filled circle shows as a sliver. Two rings show as two outlines, and two plus signs as a thicker cross with four tips. Cleveland chose the circle and plus for this reason.
Detection · Drawbacks

Drawbacks Open symbols keep overlapping points visible at the cost of less ink per point.

Small symbols fade

An open circle has less ink than a filled one. At radius 2 px it reads as a grey speck; increase the size along with switching.

Hue is stronger

Hue is a preattentive feature (slide 3); circle versus plus needs a closer look. Where colour is available, open symbols in two hues combine both.

Exact ties stay hidden

Two rows at the same position draw one symbol whatever its shape. Open circle and plus still leave 28 of 1,020 invisible. Jitter or a count label shows them.

Banking to 45° · Concept

Concept A line chart shows rate of change as slope, and the eye compares slopes as angles. The aspect ratio of the box sets those angles.

BLUE METAL, the 25th and 75th percentile month-on-month slopes (2.26 and 8.163, range per span)
The second slope is 3.6 times the first. In a square box both lines are near vertical and 16.9° apart; in the flatter box they are 34.5° apart.
Slopes are measured with time scaled to 0 to 1 across the 264 months and the close scaled to 0 to 1 across its range, so a slope of 1 is the box diagonal in a square box.

Banking to 45° · Mechanism: why 45°

Mechanism In a box of height/width a, slopes s₁ and s₂ draw at atan(a·s₁) and atan(a·s₂). The angle between them peaks at a = 1/√(s₁s₂) = 0.233, where the two angles add to 90°.

the two BLUE METAL slopes of the concept slide browser JS

Differentiate atan(a·s₂) − atan(a·s₁) in a and set it to zero: a²s₁s₂ = 1. Then the two angles are 27.7° and 62.3°, centred on 45°. Cleveland's banking puts the typical slope there.
Banking to 45° · Mechanism continued: median absolute slope

Mechanism continued 1 Cleveland's rule: choose the aspect ratio that draws the median absolute slope at 45°. On BLUE METAL that is 0.226, close to the 0.233 from the two quartile slopes.

b = pd.read_csv('BLUE METAL.csv')
b['d'] = pd.to_datetime(b.HistoryDate, format='%d %b %Y')
close = b.sort_values('d').set_index('d').CLOSE.resample('ME').last().dropna()
x = np.arange(len(close)) / (len(close) - 1)          # time to 0..1
y = (close - close.min()) / (close.max() - close.min()) # close to 0..1
s = np.abs(np.diff(y) / np.diff(x))                     # 263 slopes
round(np.median(s), 3), round(1 / np.median(s), 3)
output
(4.416, 0.226)
fig.update_layout(width=1000, height=226)      # banked for this series

Why the median

A few crash months have huge slopes. The median ignores them, so the bulk of the segments sit near 45°.

Plot area, not figure

The ratio applies to the plotting rectangle. Plotly margins and the legend add to width and height, so set margins before sizing.

Banking to 45° · Drawbacks

Drawbacks The banked shape depends on which slopes the reader should compare. The 12-month moving average of BLUE METAL banks at 0.493, the raw closes at 0.226.

BLUE METAL, month-end close

Long series become strips

264 months banked is a box 226 px high for every 1000 px wide. Cleveland cuts such a series into stacked panels.

Two scales, two shapes

Month-to-month wiggles and the long trend bank to different ratios. Draw both, or say which one the chart is for.

Shape can be chosen to persuade

A flat box calms a series and a tall one alarms it. Banking ties the shape to the data, and the caption should say it was banked.

Perceived correlation · Drawbacks

Drawbacks A judged correlation moves with choices made by whoever drew the chart. Print r and let the cloud fill the plotting region.

Axis ranges

A fixed range shared across years, or one outlier stretching an axis, shrinks the cloud and makes it look tighter.

Axis scale

On a log x axis the 2007 cloud has r = 0.809; on a linear axis the same countries give 0.679. The log axis draws a different relationship, and r measures the one drawn.

The number

Put r in the title or an annotation. The eye can rank two clouds; it cannot read 0.81 from one.
