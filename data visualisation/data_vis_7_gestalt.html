Gestalt · Concept

Concept The eye groups marks before it reads them. The same 24 dots read as a grid, as rows or as columns, and only the gaps changed.

the same 24 dots, three spacings
The grouping arrives before any measuring of gaps, and every reading of a chart starts from it.

Gestalt

German for shape or form. The principles describe which marks the eye puts together.

In a chart

Legends, colours, panels and lines all tell the eye which marks belong together.

This lecture

Each principle is used to fix a chart from lectures 2 to 6.

Gestalt · history

Where the principles come from Max Wertheimer, Kurt Koffka and Wolfgang Köhler worked them out in Germany between 1912 and the 1930s. The two red entries were added in the 1990s.

dates of the founding papers

Enclosure and connection

Stephen Palmer added both in the 1990s. Charts use them more than any other principle: panels, shaded bands, card borders and lines.

The slogan "the whole is greater than the sum of its parts" misquotes Koffka. He wrote that the whole is other than the sum of its parts: something different, not something more.

Proximity · Concept

Concept Marks that sit close together are seen as a group, even when they look different from each other.

fifteen identical dots, two arrangements

Grouping without colour

All 15 dots are the same colour and size. Distance alone makes the three groups.

In charts

A label near its line, related KPI cards close together, unrelated panels further apart.

Proximity · Example 1: legend or labels

Example With a legend, reading a line means matching its colour against a box at the edge. A label at the end of each line puts the name next to the data.

mean life expectancy by continent, 1952 to 2007, gapminder (lecture 5)
fig.update_layout(showlegend=False)
for t in fig.data:
    fig.add_annotation(x=2007, y=t.y[-1], text=t.name, xanchor='left', showarrow=False)
Proximity · Example 2: the lecture 2 dashboard

Example continued 1 With one gap size everywhere, the four KPIs and the two charts read as six equal boxes. A tight KPI row and a wider gap above the charts make two groups.

sales.parquet: 1,316,165 total sales, 500 orders
dbc.Row([...kpi cards...], className='g-2 mb-5')   # g-2: small gutter inside, mb-5: big gap below
Similarity · Concept

Concept Marks that share a colour, shape or size are seen as one group, even when they sit apart.

32 marks on an even grid

Even spacing, still rows

The right grid has equal gaps in both directions. Colour alone makes the rows.

In charts

One colour for one category, the same in every panel. The legend then has to be read once.

Similarity · Example: the colour of Africa in three charts

Example px hands out colours in the order the categories first appear in the data. Sort the data differently and Africa is blue in one chart, orange in the next and green in the third.

trace colours read from fig.data of three gapminder charts
The line chart lists continents alphabetically, the bar chart by 2007 mean, the scatter in file order (Asia first: Afghanistan).
COLORS = {'Africa': '#636efa', 'Americas': '#EF553B', 'Asia': '#00cc96',
          'Europe': '#ab63fa', 'Oceania': '#FFA15A'}
px.bar(d, x='continent', y='lifeExp', color='continent', color_discrete_map=COLORS)
Similarity · Drawbacks

Drawbacks Similarity by colour works for a handful of groups. px's default palette has 10 colours, so with 142 countries Afghanistan shares its blue with 14 others.

f = px.line(g, x='year', y='lifeExp', color='country')
blue = f.data[0].line.color
[t.name for t in f.data if t.line.color == blue]
output
['Afghanistan', 'Benin', 'Canada', "Cote d'Ivoire", 'Equatorial Guinea', 'Guatemala', 'Iran', 'Korea, Rep.', 'Mauritania', 'Netherlands', 'Peru', 'Senegal', 'Sudan', 'Tunisia', 'Zambia']

15 countries, one blue

Colour now groups countries that have nothing in common. The legend cannot tell them apart.

What to do instead

Colour the continent, or grey everything and colour one country. Figure and ground, later in this lecture.

Colour-blind readers

Red and green pairs fail for the most common colour blindness. Palettes are lecture 9.

Enclosure · Concept

Concept Marks inside a shared boundary are seen as a group. Palmer showed in 1992 that a shared region wins over closer spacing.

common region, after Palmer (1992)

Also called common region

Not in the 1923 list. Added to the principles 69 years later.

In charts

Facet panels, dbc.Card borders round KPIs, a shaded band over a period, a box round a cluster.

Enclosure · Example: a shaded period

Example Rwanda falls to 23.6 years in 1992 while the Africa mean is 53.6. A shaded band ties the fall to the civil war of 1990 to 1994.

life expectancy, Rwanda and mean of 52 African countries, gapminder
Gapminder has one row every 5 years, so the band starts between the 1987 and 1992 points.
fig.add_vrect(x0=1990, x1=1994, fillcolor='grey', opacity=0.2, line_width=0)
Connection · Concept

Concept Marks joined by a line are seen as one object. A connecting line wins over shared colour and over closer spacing.

uniform connectedness, after Palmer and Rock (1994)

Why line charts work

The line turns twelve separate points into one country, one object to follow.

In charts

Line charts, slope charts, dumbbells joining two values of one country.

Connection · Example: five countries over time

Example As dots, the 60 points of five countries mix where they cross. Joined by country, China's fall from 50.5 to 44.5 years in 1962 stands out.

life expectancy, India, China, Brazil, Nigeria, Japan, gapminder
px.line(five, x='year', y='lifeExp', color='country', markers=True)
Connection · Drawbacks

Drawbacks A line says the points are one object that changes along the axis. Across five continents in alphabetical order, it draws a trend that does not exist.

mean life expectancy in 2007 by continent
The rise from Africa to Americas is an artefact of the alphabet. Use a line only when the x axis has an order: time, dose, size.
Continuity · Concept

Concept The eye follows smooth paths and reads marks along a line or curve as one sequence.

good continuation, Wertheimer (1923)

Crossing lines

Where two lines cross, the eye keeps each on its own smooth path.

In charts

Bars sorted by value form one descending edge. Panels aligned on a shared axis let the eye run across them.

Continuity · Example: sorted bars

Example Sorted, the bar tops form one falling edge from Oceania at 80.7 to Africa at 54.8, and the ranking reads in one pass.

mean life expectancy in 2007 by continent, gapminder
fig.update_xaxes(categoryorder='total descending')
Closure · Concept

Concept The eye completes an incomplete outline and sees the whole shape.

closure

Nothing is drawn

The circle on the right has no outline. The 12 dots imply it.

In charts

The plot area can lose its box and most of its grid lines. The axis labels are enough to close the frame.

Closure · Example: the 2007 scatter without its frame

Example With the frame, the grey background and the grid lines removed, the 142 countries still read as one rising band. The axis labels are enough for the eye to close the plot area.

gdpPercap (log) against lifeExp, 142 countries, 2007, gapminder
fig.update_layout(template='plotly_white'); fig.update_xaxes(showgrid=False); fig.update_yaxes(showgrid=False)
Figure-ground · Concept

Concept The eye splits a scene into a figure that stands out and a ground behind it. Contrast decides which is which.

figure and ground

Ground is not wasted

The grey context shows what the figure is compared against.

In charts

Everything grey, the one series that makes the point in colour. The project rubric asks for one highlight colour.

Figure-ground · Example: 142 lines

Example Coloured by continent, 142 country lines form five tangled colours. Every line is figure, so none of them is.

life expectancy, every country, 1952 to 2007, gapminder
Rwanda, Cambodia and China each fall sharply somewhere in this chart. Finding them takes a search.
Figure-ground · Example: one highlight

Example continued 1 Grey all 142 lines and colour one. The chosen country becomes the figure and the rest becomes the ground it is read against.

highlight one country
The other 141 lines stay as grey ground. They show what is normal; the red line shows the exception.

Symmetry · Concept and example

Concept Marks that mirror each other about an axis are seen as one balanced whole. Back-to-back bars use it to compare two groups across a shared centre.

symmetry
mean life expectancy by continent, 1952 and 2007, gapminder
Africa gains 15.67 years, Oceania 11.46. The mirror makes each row one comparison.
A symmetric layout reads as stable. A dashboard does not need it; aligned edges, the next section, matter more.
Common fate · Concept

Concept Marks that move in the same direction at the same time are seen as one group, wherever they sit.

sixteen dots browser JS

Motion groups

The red and grey dots are mixed on screen. When the red ones move in step, they become one group.

In the lecture 6 bubble chart

From 1987 to 2002 the African mean moves from 53.34 to 53.33 while the other four continents rise. Played as frames, Africa's bubbles hang back together.

Use sparingly

An animation shows one frame at a time. A reader cannot compare 1952 with 2007 without a static chart beside it.

Prägnanz · Concept

Concept The eye reads a pattern as the simplest shape that fits it. Prägnanz is German for conciseness.

the law of good figure

In the 2007 scatter

142 points are read as one rising band, the simplest shape that fits them. On a log x axis, r = 0.81.

The cost

A simple shape hides the points that break it. Botswana earns $12,570 a head and lives to 50.7, 22 years below the band at its income.

Space, weight and hierarchy

Negative space, visual weight and hierarchy Empty space separates groups, and size, darkness and position decide what is read first.

twelve equal boxes against one heavy element and three light ones

Negative space

The gap between groups is proximity working in reverse. It is what makes the groups.

Visual weight

Larger, darker and more saturated elements pull the eye first.

Hierarchy

One primary element, a few secondary, the rest quiet. Top left is read first in a left-to-right script.

Dashboard · the project

Checking a project dashboard against the rubric The rubric line: "Gestalt principles used on purpose: grouping by proximity and colour, alignment across panels, one highlight colour for the point of the plot."

  1. Point at each group on the dashboard and name what makes it a group: a gap, a colour or a box.
  2. Print {t.name: t.marker.color for t in fig.data} for every figure. The same category must give the same colour in all of them.
  3. Check that panels in one row share a top edge and that charts with the same measure share an axis range.
  4. Name the one point of each chart and check that it is the only element in a saturated colour.
