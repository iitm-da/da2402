Misleading charts · where this lecture sits

Misleading charts Lectures 8 to 10 covered what the eye groups, reads and colours. This lecture covers the ways a chart misstates its data, and the redraw for each.

the sections of this lecture

Fool, then fix

Each chart appears first as it was published or as someone would draw it. The class reads it, then the slide shows what the numbers say and redraws it.

Measured

Each trick gets a number on real data: a lie factor, a count, or a rate before and after.

Lie factor · Concept

Concept Tufte (1983) measures distortion as the ratio of the change a graphic shows to the change in the numbers behind it.

Tufte, The Visual Display of Quantitative Information, 1983

Measurable

Every distortion in this lecture gets a number: a lie factor where the chart encodes a change, a count where it does not.

Where it applies

Any chart that encodes a change as a length, an area or a height. Dual axes, sorting and missing data need a count instead.

Published · Example 1: Fox Business, 2012

Example The top US income tax rate if the Bush tax cuts expired: 35.0% to 39.6%, a rise of 13.1%. On an axis from 34 the second bar is drawn 5.6 times as tall: a lie factor of 35.0.

recreated from the broadcast figures: "Top tax rate", 35% now, 39.6% from 1 Jan 2013
data: 39.6 / 35.0 = 1.131 drawn: (39.6-34)/(35-34) = 5.60 lie factor (5.60-1)/(0.131) = 35.0

Redraw

Bars from 0. The rise is 4.6 points on a base of 35, and now it looks like it.

Ask the room first: how much higher is the new rate?

Scale · Truncated axis: when it is fine

A line chart can start above zero A bar encodes its value as a length from zero, so its axis has to start at zero. A line encodes values as positions, and its axis can start where the data starts.

264 month-end closes, 2004-01 to 2025-12
This axis starts at 0 and the early years flatten into the floor. An axis from 500 would show the same shape with more detail, since the eye reads a line by its position.

Bars

Length from zero is the encoding. Truncating changes the encoding.

Lines and dots

Position against the axis is the encoding. Zoom to the data, and label the axis start.

Scale · Example 4: linear and log axes

Example continued 3 On a linear axis China's line stays flat until 1990. Switch to log: China grew 12.39 times from 1952 to 2007 and the US 3.07 times.

browser JS
gdpPercap, gapminder, 1952 to 2007
Both axes are honest. They answer different questions: linear shows the gap in dollars, log shows how fast each one grew.
Scale · Mechanism: the log axis

Mechanism On a log axis the slope is the growth rate. China's line is steeper because it grew 4.68% a year against the US's 2.06%.

log10 spacing
rate = (v_2007 / v_1952) ** (1 / 55) - 1
# China 4.68%   India 2.77%   US 2.06%
px.line(df, x='year', y='gdpPercap', color='country', log_y=True)

Drawback

A reader who misses the word log reads a tenfold gap as a small one. Label the ticks with real values (1,000, 10,000) and say log in the axis title.

Selection · Test: bin-width rules

Test Three standard rules for the bin width, run on the same 142 values. Sturges gives 3 peaks, Scott and Freedman-Diaconis give 2; red marks each peak.

k     = ceil(log2(n)) + 1          # Sturges: 9 bins
h     = 3.49 * sd * n ** (-1/3)    # Scott, sd = 12.07
h     = 2 * IQR * n ** (-1/3)      # Freedman-Diaconis, IQR = 19.25
np.histogram_bin_edges(lifeExp, bins='fd')
The three rules differ by 3.3 years of width and by one peak. Freedman-Diaconis uses the IQR, so outliers do not widen its bins.
Selection · Example 4: smoothing

Example continued 3 BLUE METAL lost 78.4% from its January 2008 peak to November 2008. A 250-day moving average shows a fall of 45.6%, and its low arrives in July 2009, eight months late.

moving average over 250 trading days
browser JS
BLUE METAL daily close, 2005 to 2010 (grey) and its moving average
close.rolling(250).mean()   # the line drawn; the worst fall is measured on it
Selection · Mechanism: the lag of a moving average

Mechanism A trailing k-day mean is centred (k−1)/2 days in the past, so a 250-day mean runs 124.5 trading days late. Centring it removes the lag and still flattens the fall to -45.6%.

eleven days, two ways to average them
close.rolling(250).mean()               # trailing: worst fall -45.6%
close.rolling(250, center=True).mean()  # centred:  worst fall -45.6%

Redraw

Plot the raw series in grey under the smooth line, and give the window length in the legend.

Aggregation · Example 2: Simpson's paradox, Berkeley 1973

Example continued 1 Berkeley graduate admissions, 1973, six largest departments: 44.5% of men admitted against 30.4% of women. Step to split by department: women's rate is higher in 4 of the 6.

Bickel, Hammel and O'Connell, Science, 1975; the six-department table in R's UCBAdmissions
ucb.groupby('Gender')[['Admitted','Applied']].sum()            # pooled
ucb.groupby(['Dept','Gender'])[['Admitted','Applied']].sum()  # split
Aggregation · Mechanism: who applied where

Mechanism Most women applied to the departments that admitted fewest. 51% of men applied to A and B, which admitted most, against 7% of women; the pooled rate mixes the departments in different proportions.

each department at its admission rate; circle area is the number of applicants
Before pooling, ask what a group mixes. Show the split, or a rate standardised to the same mix of departments.
Aggregation · Test: standardised rates

Test Apply each sex's six department rates to the same applicant mix. Men's rate becomes 38.7% and women's 43.0%: the gap reverses.

Berkeley 1973, six departments
mix  = applicants_by_dept / 4526   # A..F: 933, 585, 918, 792, 584, 714
rate = (dept_rate[g] * mix).sum()
Standardising answers one question: what would each group's rate be on the same mix? Show it beside the pooled rate, and say which mix.

Encoding · Reveal: missing data on a map

Reveal Gapminder gives both Koreas the code KOR (12 rows each). Plotly can draw only one of them at KOR, and North Korea's shape is left in the grey used for countries gapminder never had.

what the reader sees, and the honest version
g.groupby('iso_alpha').country.nunique().loc[lambda s: s > 1]
output
iso_alpha KOR 2 Name: country, dtype: int64
A missing value drawn as a colour, even grey, reads as a value. Give it its own pattern and its own legend entry, and say in the caption how many countries have none.
