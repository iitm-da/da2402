Stacked bars · Drawbacks

Drawbacks Only the bottom segment starts at zero. Europe grew from 418 million to 586 million, and the 100% chart shows its band shrinking.

One shared baseline

Asia, at the bottom, is read against the axis. Every other segment floats on the ones below it, so its length is read by eye.

Shares hide totals

The 100% chart cannot show that the world grew 2.6 times. Europe's shrinking band is a falling share of a growing total.

Order matters

Change the stacking order and a different continent gets the baseline. Put the one you want compared at the bottom.

Treemap · Concept and example

Concept Nested rectangles: each continent is a rectangle split into its countries, and area is proportional to population.

2007 population, continent then country
px.treemap(g07, path=[px.Constant('world'), 'continent', 'country'], values='pop')
Treemap · Mechanism: the squarified layout

Mechanism The squarified layout fills the box one strip at a time and keeps adding rectangles to a strip while that makes their shapes closer to squares.

  1. Sort the children by value, largest first.
  2. Start a strip along the shorter side of the free space.
  3. Add the next child to the strip if that brings the worst width-to-height ratio in the strip closer to 1.
  4. Otherwise close the strip, shrink the free space and start a new one.
  5. Repeat inside each rectangle for the next level of the hierarchy.

Why squares

Areas of near-square rectangles are easier to compare than long slivers, and labels fit in them.

148 rectangles

px.treemap builds 1 world, 5 continents and 142 countries: the ids in fig.data[0].ids.

Bruls, Huizing and van Wijk published the squarified algorithm in 2000. The figure on the last slide was drawn with it.
Sunburst · Example

Example The same hierarchy as rings: continents inside, countries outside, angle proportional to population. China and India together take 38.9% of the circle.

2007 population
px.sunburst(g07, path=['continent', 'country'], values='pop')

Same data, same call

Treemap and sunburst take the same path and values. Only the geometry differs.

Rings read as levels

The distance from the centre is the depth in the hierarchy, which the treemap shows only by nesting.

Treemap and sunburst · Drawbacks

Drawbacks In the treemap above, 122 of the 142 countries get a rectangle too small to carry a label. 48 countries hold under 0.1% of the population each.

Area and angle

Both charts ask the reader to compare areas or angles, which lecture 8 shows are read less accurately than lengths.

Small parts vanish

Hover in plotly recovers the small leaves one at a time. On a printed slide they stay unreadable.

No negatives, no overlap

Values must be positive parts of a whole. A country in two continents, or a negative GDP change, cannot be drawn.

A sorted bar chart of the 20 largest countries answers "which countries are biggest" more exactly than either.

Candlestick · Mechanism

Mechanism The body runs from open to close and the wick from low to high. An OHLC bar draws the same four numbers as ticks.

Decomposition · Example

Example seasonal_decompose splits 168 monthly closes into trend, a 12-month seasonal pattern and a residual.

Decomposition · Drawbacks: a season that is not there

Drawbacks The seasonal pattern spans 270.68 index points. The residual's standard deviation is 332.12. The routine returns a seasonal pattern for any series, whether or not it has one.

monthJanFebMarAprMayJunJulAugSepOctNovDec
seasonal-26-154-94+19+24-4+53+37+40-24+12+117

Why it appears

The seasonal part is the average of each calendar month after removing the trend. 14 Februaries of noise average to something other than zero.

Test it

Compare the seasonal range with the residual spread, or decompose the series with its months shuffled and see whether the pattern survives.

Sales, rainfall and electricity demand have calendar seasons, and on such series decomposition is worth running.
3D scatter · Drawbacks

Drawbacks A 3D scatter on a flat screen is a projection. The same 142 points look like a line, a cloud or a band depending on the angle.

log gdp, lifeExp, log pop for 2007, three rotations of one px.scatter_3d

Depth is guessed

A point near the viewer and a point far away land on the same pixel. Rotating the plot recovers depth. A printed slide shows one fixed angle.

Use instead

The scatter matrix shows the same 3 pairs with exact positions, and colour or size can carry a third variable.

px.scatter_3d(g07, x='lgdp', y='lifeExp', z='lpop', color='continent')
