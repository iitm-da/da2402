Colour · Concept: three kinds of palette

Concept The palette follows the data: an ordered measure gets a sequential ramp, a measure with a meaningful midpoint gets a diverging one, unordered groups get distinct hues.

ΔE is the straight-line distance between two colours in CIELAB, the CIE76 formula. A distance near 2 is close to the smallest difference people notice side by side; the colour-blindness slides use the same measure.

Colour · Mechanism: lightness

Mechanism The eye reads order from lightness. Viridis rises from L* 14.9 to 90.9 without a dip; jet climbs to 95.2 at 0.625 and falls back to 25.4.

L* computed from each colormap at 33 points, matplotlib 3.10
Red: jet. Green: viridis. Jet's brightest band is yellow-green near the middle, so the middle of the data looks like its peak.
colormapL* minL* maxmonotonic
viridis14.990.9yes
jet12.995.2no
rainbow40.191.5no
L* is CIELAB lightness, 0 black to 100 white, computed from sRGB with the D65 white point.
Colour · Mechanism: jet without its hues

Mechanism continued 1 Print jet in grey and position 0.06 (#0000c8, L* 24.3) matches position 1 (#800000, L* 25.4): the lowest and the highest values look the same.

top: jet; bottom: each colour replaced by a grey of the same L*
A greyscale printout, a washed-out projector and a colour-blind reader all lean on lightness. Jet gives them a ramp that goes up and comes back down.

Assumptions A palette choice assumes things about the reader and the screen that the analyst does not control.

Colour · Drawbacks

Drawbacks Colour gives order and grouping. It gives a poor reading of value, and each chart can spend it on one variable.

Values

Lecture 8 ranked colour below position, length, angle and area for reading a quantity. Print the number when it matters.

One variable

Colour by continent and the same chart cannot also colour by income. Use a facet or a second chart.

The legend

Every colour costs a look at the legend. Direct labels on the lines or bars remove the lookup.

Honesty · Concept: the lie factor

Concept Tufte (1983) measures distortion as the ratio of the change a graphic shows to the change in the numbers behind it.

Tufte, The Visual Display of Quantitative Information, 1983

Measurable

Every distortion in this section gets a number: a lie factor where the chart encodes a change, a count where it does not.

Seven cases

Truncated bars, dual axes, cumulative lines, pictograms, aspect ratio, time windows and missing data.

Honesty · Example 1: truncated bars

Example Europe's 2007 mean life expectancy is 1.055 times the Americas'. Start the axis at 70 and Europe's bar is drawn 2.12 times as tall: a lie factor of 20.4.

axis from 70
Bars of 3.61 and 7.65: the graphic shows a 111.9% difference.
axis from 0
The data has a 5.5% difference. 111.9 / 5.5 = 20.4.
px.bar(means, x='continent', y='lifeExp', range_y=[0, 85])
Honesty · Truncated axis: when it is fine

A line chart can start above zero A bar encodes its value as a length from zero, so its axis has to start at zero. A line encodes values as positions, and its axis can start where the data starts.

264 month-end closes, 2004-01 to 2025-12
This axis starts at 0 and the early years flatten into the floor. An axis from 500 would show the same shape with more detail, since the eye reads a line by its position.

Bars

Length from zero is the encoding. Truncating changes the encoding.

Lines and dots

Position against the axis is the encoding. Zoom to the data, and label the axis start.

Example continued 1 India, 1952 to 2007: the same two series, the same left axis. The right axis alone decides whether income overtakes health in 2007 or never comes close.

right axis 500 to 2,500

Where two lines cross on a dual-axis chart is set by two arbitrary ranges. The lie factor does not apply here: the crossing exists only in the drawing.
Honesty · dual axes redrawn

Dual axes redrawn as an index Divide each series by its 1952 value. From 1952 to 2007 India's life expectancy grew to 173% of its start and GDP per capita to 448%, on one axis.

both series on a single axis, 1952 = 100
ind['lifeExp_idx'] = 100 * ind.lifeExp / ind.lifeExp.iloc[0]

Honesty · Example 4: pictograms

Example continued 3 World population grew 2.60 times from 1952 to 2007. An icon scaled 2.60 times in height and width covers 6.74 times the ink: a lie factor of 3.6.

gapminder total population, 142 countries
Effect in the data: 2.60 − 1 = 1.60. Effect in the icon's area: 6.74 − 1 = 5.74. Lie factor 5.74 / 1.60 = 3.6.
Lecture 5 measured the same trap on bubbles. Plotly Express sizes bubbles by area, the honest choice for a circle; a hand-scaled icon gets both dimensions stretched.
Honesty · Example 5: aspect ratio

Example continued 4 The same 264 months drawn at three shapes. The median segment of BLUE METAL climbs at 77.2° in a square box and at 44.9° when the box is 0.226 times as high as it is wide.

square: median slope 77.2°
height = width / 2: 65.6°
banked, height = 0.226 × width: 44.9°
Cleveland, McGill and McGill (1988) found slopes are compared best near 45°, and proposed choosing the aspect ratio so the typical segment sits there: banking to 45°. Here the median absolute slope sets it.
fig.update_layout(width=1000, height=226)      # banked for this series

Honesty · Reveal: missing data on a map

Reveal Gapminder gives both Koreas the code KOR (12 rows each). Plotly can draw only one of them at KOR, and North Korea's shape is left in the grey used for countries gapminder never had.

what the reader sees, and the honest version
g.groupby('iso_alpha').country.nunique().loc[lambda s: s > 1]
output
iso_alpha KOR 2 Name: country, dtype: int64
A missing value drawn as a colour, even grey, reads as a value. Give it its own pattern and its own legend entry, and say in the caption how many countries have none.
